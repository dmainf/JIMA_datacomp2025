//Retrieval_Augmented_Time_Series_Forecasting//

TSFM	Time-Series Foundation Model	e.g. Chronos, Moirai
That's why use RAF
* time-series data is inherently dynamic, heterogeneous and context-dependent
* time-series exhibit complex pattern


Rule to make good RAF
* Euclidean distance within distinct norms is better than cosine similarity
* Transformer with stride length 1 is good
* larger models exhibit faster convergence
* on longer-context, l2 and Pearson's correlation is good
* on shorter-context, cosine similarity and Pearson's correlation is good
	-> direction is more important than scale
* Loss function is WQL(Weighed Quantile Loss) or MASE


Methodology of RAF
1. 20% test, 80% retrieval, embedding series and normalize series
2. Identify retrieved context, calculating l2 each time-step
3. Concatenate original and retrieved apply additive offset to enforce continuity
	retrieved context ＋ retrieved future ＋ original context(un-embedding)


Result
* larger model gain better
* classic non-attention model yield little and even hurt performance
* Fine-turning for TSFM added RAF is good
* On dataset with Gaussian noise and missing-at-random, RAF outperform the baseline
